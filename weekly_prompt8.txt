Question:
Colormaps are used extensively with scientific data. 
How are these applied when visualizing measurable phenomenon 
like temperatures or rainfall? Reference your textbook or the 
internet. Cite examples that you find interesting.


Answer:
Colormaps are used to visually distinguish between differences 
in data points. They can be used to plot 
points that are keyed in from research (like amount of rainfall 
during a period of time or specifically looking at water buildup 
levels in specific spots) and colormap them to form a 
color coded map and key showing which color and hue equates to what 
value. Multiple of these maps can then be looked at to distinguish 
between areas that have consistent heavy rainfall to plan for  
sturdy infrastructure and evac plans, among other things. 

My understanding didn't quite click while I was reading through 
the book's examples, but I realized after doing a little research 
that these look very similar to radar maps. Seeing them in that way 
helped solidify their usefulness. They are very important for research 
and their ability to quickly map data in a way that is easily understandable. 
And they have the ability to parse through very large amounts of data with 
easy control over style.
